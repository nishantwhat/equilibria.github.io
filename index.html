🦠 2020: Demand Surge

COVID-19 caused an unprecedented demand spike. Governments, hospitals, and consumers competed for masks. Demand curve shifted massively right. Price tripled overnight.

💉 2021–22: The Turn

Vaccines reduced fear. Mandates lifted. Consumers stopped stockpiling. Demand softened rapidly. Yet factories had expanded capacity — setting up oversupply.

📉 2023: The Collapse

Demand settled at a permanently lower level. Hundreds of new factories (supply) entered during the boom. Result: Classic demand-led price collapse. Prices fell 74% from peak.

💡 Key Economic Concept

Demand shift ≠ movement along the curve. When consumer preferences change, the entire curve moves. The equilibrium price and quantity both change — even if producers don't change their costs.

The Transition Playbook — 4 Steps
1
Market signal received: Price of masks falls below break-even (₹110 vs cost of ₹140). The factory owner compares alternatives — fashion wear shows a ₹260+ margin opportunity.
2
Capital retooling: Mask welding machines are sold or repurposed. Industrial sewing machines are leased. Initial retooling cost: ~₹18 lakh (amortized over 3 years).
3
Labour retraining: Workers attend a 6-week intensive stitching and pattern-cutting course subsidized through PMKVY. Some workers leave; new skilled tailors are hired.
4
New production begins: First fashion batch ships in Month 3. By Month 6, the factory operates at 70% of target capacity. Economies of scale kick in below.

🔭 Long-Run Perspective

In the long run, firms enter and exit markets until economic profit → zero. The fashion boom will attract new competitors, eventually compressing margins again. The cycle restarts — which is why continuous innovation matters.

🎯 Supply Shift — Recap

A supply shift (S₁→S₂) means producers are willing to supply more at every price. When demand holds steady, a rightward supply shift lowers equilibrium price and raises quantity. In masks, demand also fell — causing an even sharper price collapse.
